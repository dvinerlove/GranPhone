# Source 45 — Older Adults' First Interactions with a Voice Assistant (JMIR)

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7840274/
- Title: Exploring How Older Adults Use a Smart Speaker–Based Voice Assistant in Their First Interactions: Qualitative Study
- Publisher: JMIR (Journal of Medical Internet Research), via PubMed Central; Kim & Choudhury, 2021
- Tier: L1 (peer-reviewed study, n=18 participants aged 74+, smart-speaker novices)
- Ownership: independent-primary (academic research)
- Fetch date: 2026-11-22 (via r.jina.ai full text)

## Key verbatim quotes

1. "The overall first response to a voice assistant was positive, thanks to the simplicity of a speech-based interaction... However, most of the follow-up reactions were unfavorable because of the difficulty in constructing a structured sentence for a command; misperceptions about how a voice assistant operates; and concerns about privacy, security, and financial burdens."

2. "many participants kept forgetting to start a command with a wake word or confused the wake word with other similar words throughout the interview."

3. "A bigger issue was that many participants struggled to compose a concise command sentence for a task. They often spoke lengthy, unstructured, and descriptive sentences, some of which even included another question within a question."

4. "Removing these misperceptions and helping them easily understand the basic concepts of how a voice assistant operates would be the first step toward lowering barriers to entry and helping novice older users better explore and facilitate its capabilities."

5. "a well-designed user interface must be intuitive enough for novice users without needing any manuals or instruction." (caveat on tutorials as a remedy)

6. "a command query should be structured in a particular way that a voice assistant can comprehend (eg, a wake word followed by a concise and definitive sentence for a task after a brief pause) was a primary challenge, making the interaction not truly conversational but rather a series of one-directional comments."

7. "One problem is associated with hearing loss, a common physical complaint in older adults... hearing loss imposes a significant challenge to the use of voice assistants for many of them." (relevant to TTS output design: rate/volume/clarity)

8. "the small size and low contrast of buttons on a mobile display have a significant negative influence on interaction performance" (cited background finding)

## Notes
- Design implications given by authors: teach how the assistant works, design for mistakes/common patterns, tailor features. Implication for GranPhone: voice/TTS helps but seniors cannot produce "Alexa-style" structured commands — free-form patience, confirmation prompts, and always-visible typed fallback needed. Voice as *output* (TTS reading screens/buttons) is lower-friction than voice *input*.
