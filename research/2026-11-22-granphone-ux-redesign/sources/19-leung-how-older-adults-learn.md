## Key verbatim quotes

1. Trial-and-error aversion: "86%, a vast majority, of younger respondents chose trial-and-error as one of their three preferred learning methods, but only 33% of older adults reported this preference."
2. Why no trial-and-error: "half of the responses (4/8) were related to negative past experiences, including frustration ('I get frustrated when it doesn't work at once!', respondent, age 72) and unwanted changes ('I... think I could mess [the device] up', respondent, age 74)."
3. Fear of irreversible harm: "Participants in our field study revealed their fear of breaking the device and the possibility of incurring unwanted charges while they tried to perform new tasks."
4. Manual preference: "63% of older respondents expressed this preference compared to less than 36% of younger respondents" — but manuals were "poorly written", "used unfamiliar terminology targeted at a more advanced level", and participants had "difficulty in mapping references to the device's user interface illustrated in the manual to the user interface on the actual device".
5. Dexterity: "older adults are more prone to making errors on mobile devices due to their relatively decreased finger dexterity."
6. Baseline inability (Ofcom 2009): older phone owners "17% could take a photo and 40% could send a text message compared to 56% and 81%" of all owners.
7. Beginner profile: "Older beginners... reported that understanding how the software works was much less important than figuring out the exact steps required to perform a task." ; they wanted resources that are "interactive", "provide demonstrations (6/6)", and "provide opportunities to practice (5/6)".
8. Stuck without guidance: "field study participants reported apprehension when they did not know what tasks to try out first and when they got stuck during trial-and-error."

## Notes
- Survey N=131 (3 age groups) + field study N=6 (50+). The single most relevant source for "a person who has literally never held a phone": novices don't explore — they need numbered steps, demonstrations, practice, and safety from irreversible actions.
