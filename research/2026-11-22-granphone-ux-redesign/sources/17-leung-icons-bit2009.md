## Key verbatim quotes

1. Abstract: "We found that our older participants did have more problems using existing mobile device icons, but that particular icon characteristics – semantically close meaning (i.e. natural, close link between depicted objects and associated function), familiar, labelled and concrete (i.e. those depicting real-world objects) – improved icon usability for them."
2. Age effect (Table 2, N=36): Identification 96% (20–39) vs 86% (65+); "Interpretation 75% (1.9%) 60% (1.9%)" — older adults correctly interpreted 60% of icons vs 75% for younger, large effect (η²=0.44).
3. Labels (Table 2): "Interpretation 45% (2.0%) 78% (1.5%) 79% (1.8%)" for icon-only vs icon+label vs label-only — adding a text label nearly doubles interpretation accuracy.
4. Worst case: older adults correctly interpreted only 39% (SD 25%) of abstract+semantically-far icons (Table 3).
5. "labels improved initial icon usability but did not help older adults more than younger ones" (H4 not supported — labels help everyone).
6. Familiarity gap: younger average familiarity 4.8/10 vs older 2.6/10.
7. Ceiling: "our younger participants had perfect identification/interpretation scores 6% of the time while our older participants scored perfectly 0% of the time."

## Notes
- N=36 controlled experiment + prior qualitative study (n=10). Direct evidence for "icons without labels fail with seniors": icon-only interpretation ~45% vs ~78–79% with label.
