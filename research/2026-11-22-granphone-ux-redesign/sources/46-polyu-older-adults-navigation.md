# Source 46 — Older Adults' Use of Mobile Devices: Usability Challenges Navigating Interfaces

- URL (OA PDF): http://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/105044/1/Li_Older_Adults_Use.pdf
- Title: Older adults' use of mobile device: usability challenges while navigating various interfaces
- Publisher: Behaviour & Information Technology (Taylor & Francis), DOI 10.1080/0144929X.2019.1622786; authors: Q. Li, Y. Luximon (PolyU). Open-access accepted manuscript.
- Tier: L1 (peer-reviewed; usability test + interviews, n=22 older adults, 19 navigation tasks)
- Ownership: independent-primary (academic research)
- Fetch date: 2026-11-22 (via r.jina.ai full text of PDF)

## Key verbatim quotes

1. "The results found that overall older adults were able to navigate using contents more effectively than menus and buttons. Participants experienced great challenges in directing their attention to the menus and buttons, understanding the meaning of icons, and interacting with these menu components. In contrast, the content-oriented navigation design performed better in understanding, navigation, and interaction."

2. "text labels for icons and buttons can also help older adults memorize functions, locations, and navigation paths"

3. Hamburger/side-drawer findings: "11 participants could not understand the meaning of hamburger icon. 9 participants could not find the entry point of side drawer at first, and 2 participants could not precisely tap the hamburger button since it was too close to the edge of the screen." Side-drawer entry task: "29.0% of breakdowns and 29.0% of complete failures."

4. "3 users could not correctly distinguish between the touchable and non-touchable text" — tappability confusion documented in seniors.

5. "designers should use simple, meaningful and distinctive icons to promote users' recognition and comprehension of assisted navigation buttons."

6. "visual differences and feedbacks between the touchable and the non-touchable text and icons should be further enhanced to facilitate older adults' process of distinguishing."

7. On elevation/shadow cues (critical for Material-style FAB/elevation): "Participants reported that they seldom noticed or tapped this button though the floating action button provides an obvious visual cue through the use of shadows. The reason may lie at the decreased visual perception influenced older adults' ability to understand the depth cues of 3D shapes." — elevation alone does NOT signal tappability to seniors; also notes older adults "preferred the use of raised buttons, which provide visual highlighting and immediate tactile feedback."

8. "older adults also encounter problems in differentiating which areas could be touched and which could not" and "even a simple gesture like tapping, swiping, scrolling, or flipping can be very difficult for them."

9. Limitation: participants had "high education level and high digital literacy" — real seniors likely worse.

## Notes
- Strongest single academic source for Q5: covers icon comprehension (hamburger fails), text labels helping memory, touchable-vs-static confusion, and the finding that shadow/elevation depth cues are NOT reliably perceived by older adults. Content-first navigation > icon menus.
