# Source 41 — Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty

- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/
- Title: Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty
- Publisher: Nielsen Norman Group (author: Kate Meyer), published 2017-09-03
- Tier: L3 (industry research/analysis; reports NN/g's own eyetracking experiment, n=71)
- Ownership: vendor-authored (NN/g's own study write-up)
- Fetch date: 2026-11-22 (via r.jina.ai full text)

## Key verbatim quotes

1. "The average amount of time was significantly higher on the weak-signifier versions than the strong-signifier versions. On average participants spent 22% more time (i.e., slower task performance) looking at the pages with weak signifiers."

2. "The average number of fixations was significantly higher on the weak-signifier versions than the strong-signifier versions. On average, people had 25% more fixations on the pages with weak signifiers."

3. "The weak-signifier versions of the pages resulted in a broader distribution of fixations across the page: people had to look around more. This result reinforced our findings that weak-signifier pages required more fixations and more time than strong-signifier ones."

4. "The problem is not that users never see a weakly signified UI element. It's that even when they do see the weak element, they don't feel confident that it is what they want, so they keep looking around the page."

5. "9 of the 24 participants who saw the weak signifier version stopped at the subheading, and never looked at the text link." (Brilliant Earth stimulus; links styled like static text)

6. Flat designs can work only when the page has: "Low information density... Traditional layout... Salient, high-contrast targets." — all three criteria, "not just one or two."

7. "We conducted a quantitative experiment using eyetracking equipment and a desktop computer. We recruited 71 general web-users to participate in the experiment."

## Notes
- Quantitative evidence that removing clickability signifiers (ghost buttons, flat links) measurably slows users and creates "click uncertainty" — direct support for strong affordances (raised/filled buttons) in GranPhone.
- Desktop/web stimuli; population = general web users, not seniors — effect likely amplified for novices (hedge accordingly).
