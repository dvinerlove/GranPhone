- Publisher: Nielsen Norman Group (author: Page Laubheimer), published 2017-03-12
- Tier: L3 (industry research/analysis blog)
- Ownership: vendor-authored (NN/g)
- Fetch date: 2026-11-22 (via r.jina.ai full text)

## Key verbatim quotes

1. "Flat designs often suffer from usability problems and cause click uncertainty because of lost signifiers. Avoid these negative side effects by consistently and clearly differentiating between clickable and unclickable elements."

2. "Never use the same visual treatment for static text and linked text. Don't use the same color for primary-action buttons and the background on a static heading. Use your visual design to clue users into what's clickable on your site and what isn't. Consistency is crucial."

3. "Make sure your buttons at least vaguely look like physical buttons."

4. "Avoid ghost buttons — text with a thin rectangular outline."

5. "Use standard, recognizable icons in links. With few exceptions, these icons should be paired with label text."

6. "Hover-triggered changes in the visual design of the element, or cursor-shape changes (e.g., into a hand) are clues that the element is clickable. Unfortunately, such clickability signifiers are weak and require interaction effort; as a result, they effectively reduce target discoverability." — hover cues don't exist on touch; touch UIs need persistent visual signifiers.

7. "There are no 'red herrings' — no false targets that look clickable, but aren't."

## Notes
- Practical checklist for signaling tappability: filled/raised buttons over ghost/outline; unique color reserved for interactive elements; icons + labels; consistent treatment. Maps directly to GranPhone card/button visual language (use Material filled buttons/cards with strong fill contrast, not just elevation — see source 46 on seniors not perceiving shadow depth cues).
